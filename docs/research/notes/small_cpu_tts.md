# Small open-source CPU TTS for Mandarin

Research date: 24 November 2026. Scope is local open weights that can speak Mandarin on a CPU-only ultrabook (Intel Core i5-8260U class: 4 cores, no usable GPU, shared with ASR). Target envelope: artifacts on the order of a few hundred MB, RTF comfortably under 1, low time-to-first-audio. Sherpa-onnx checkpoint *selection* is out of scope; an ONNX file that also happens to have a sherpa wrapper is in scope when the upstream model is independently packaged. Cloud APIs are excluded, not recommended.

Every size and RTF below is quoted from a retrieved page. Where two pages disagree, both are listed. No i5-8260U measurement was found for any model.

## For each candidate: repo, license, parameter count, download size, sample rate, languages, whether Mandarin is first-class, streaming, ONNX/int8, and published CPU RTF

### Takeaway

The only stacks that are simultaneously open-weight, Mandarin-capable, a few hundred MB or less, and ONNX-friendly are Kokoro-82M v1.1-zh, official Piper zh_CN medium voices (license-encumbered), PaddleSpeech FastSpeech2 (2022-era, but the only i5-U-class RTF), MeloTTS Chinese (MIT and explicit Chinese-English mix, Pi 4 not realtime), the 18 MB synthetic distill tiny-voca-zh (21 November 2026), and MOSS-TTS-Nano (100M, streaming ONNX, but the published fp32 tree and one CPU port are heavier than this box should share with ASR). KittenTTS, NeuTTS, Supertonic, Pocket TTS official, StyleTTS2-lite, and OuteTTS do not clear the Mandarin-plus-small-CPU bar. GPT-SoVITS and Bert-VITS2 do not have a demonstrated tiny open distill.

### Cited Findings

#### Piper (rhasspy/piper-voices)

- Code repository page `https://github.com/rhasspy/piper` returned no README body and no LICENSE text in this pass (the fetched landing page showed 0 stars and only links). A secondary writeup states the repository is MIT and that each voice is an ONNX file of about 20–100 MB plus a json config, with espeak-ng or piper-phonemize as the phonemizer. — [Jason's Study, Piper](https://estelledc.github.io/study/projects/piper/)
- Official voice tree `rhasspy/piper-voices`, `zh/zh_CN`, lists three voices: chaowen, huayan, xiao_ya. The folder is shown as 211 MB. — [Hugging Face tree zh/zh_CN](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/tree/main/zh/zh_CN)
- `zh_CN-huayan-medium.onnx` is 63.2 MB; the voice directory is 63.4 MB. Model card: zh_CN, 1 speaker, quality medium, sample rate 22,050 Hz, dataset `https://github.com/PlayVoice/HuaYan_TTS`, license "Unknown", "Finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice (medium quality)." — [huayan medium tree](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/tree/main/zh/zh_CN/huayan/medium); [huayan MODEL_CARD](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/raw/main/zh/zh_CN/huayan/medium/MODEL_CARD)
- A third-party sherpa bundle mirror lists Chinese (CN) sizes as chaowen 58 MB, huayan 64 MB, xiao_ya 58 MB. — [4-alokk/piper-voices](https://huggingface.co/4-alokk/piper-voices)
- chaowen medium model card (checkpoint dataset repo, updated in the days before 24 Nov 2026): language zh_CN, 1 speaker, quality medium, sample rate 22,050 Hz, dataset license CC0 at `https://github.com/OHF-Voice/voice-datasets`, "Finetuned from the U.S. English hifi_tts_92 base model (medium quality; the voice of Cori Samuel, Hi-Fi TTS reader 92)", phonemes are pinyin via g2pW, "Requires Piper 1.5 or higher." — [chaowen MODEL_CARD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rhasspy/piper-checkpoints/blob/main/zh/zh_CN/chaowen/medium/MODEL_CARD)
- Loudness.ai, 15 October 2026, catalog revision c10ece1: 177 official Piper files in 53 languages. 102 were excluded from the commercial set, 76 because they were fine-tuned from Lessac (non-commercial recordings) and 26 because their own licence is non-commercial or research-only. The exclusion table lists `zh_CN-chaowen-medium` as "Built on the Lessac voice" with stated licence CC0, `zh_CN-huayan-medium` as "Built on the Lessac voice" with stated licence "Not stated", and `zh_CN-xiao_ya-medium` as "Non-commercial or research-only licence" / "DataBaker, non-commercial". The same article's per-language score table still shows "Chinese (China) | 1 | Huayan | 79.6 | 79.6" inside the commercially usable study. Those two tables conflict. Scores are UTMOS22 naturalness plus Whisper large-v3 clarity, and the author warns they are not comparable across languages. — [Loudness.ai Piper voice study](https://loudness.ai/research/piper-tts-voices-ranked/)
- No Chinese-voice RTF was found. The closest official-style numbers are sherpa-onnx on a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B Rev 1.5 for *English* Piper medium/similar ONNX models: `en_US-glados` (61 MB class) RTF 0.812 / 0.480 / 0.391 / 0.349 at 1/2/3/4 threads; `en_US-libritts_r-medium` (75 MB) RTF 0.790 / 0.493 / 0.392 / 0.357. — [sherpa-onnx VITS pretrained models](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/vits.html)
- The secondary Piper writeup says Pi 4 medium RTF is "远小于 1" and that a 10-character Chinese sentence on Pi 4 was "不到 1 秒" including ~200 ms startup. Those are not a published RTF table. It also says espeak-ng Chinese G2P is weak on polyphones, neutral tone, and erhua, and that long-form speech sounds mechanical. The chaowen card above has since switched that voice to pinyin/g2pW, so the espeak critique may not apply to Piper 1.5 chaowen. — [Jason's Study, Piper](https://estelledc.github.io/study/projects/piper/)
- English words inside Chinese sentences: not stated on the huayan or chaowen cards retrieved here.
- Streaming: the same secondary page describes writing audio while synthesizing. No official streaming API quote was retrieved. int8 Piper Chinese ONNX was not found.
- piper-phonemize / espeak-ng: phonemizer frontends, not neural TTS. No Mandarin MOS for espeak-ng's own formant synthesizer was retrieved. Judged as a fallback phonemizer only; not a spoken-reply voice.

#### MeloTTS (myshell-ai/MeloTTS)

- Repo `myshell-ai/MeloTTS`. Languages include English (several accents), Spanish, French, Chinese (mix EN), Japanese, Korean. README: "The Chinese speaker supports mixed Chinese and English" and "Fast enough for CPU real-time inference." License: "This library is under MIT License, which means it is free for both commercial and non-commercial use." No parameter count, byte size, sample rate, or RTF number on that page. Inference example is PyTorch (`from melo.api import TTS`, `device='cpu'`) with the mixed sentence "我最近在学习machine learning，希望能够在未来的artificial intelligence领域有所建树。" — [GitHub myshell-ai/MeloTTS](https://github.com/myshell-ai/MeloTTS/); [HF myshell-ai/MeloTTS-Chinese](https://huggingface.co/myshell-ai/MeloTTS-Chinese)
- sherpa-onnx export of that Chinese checkpoint: `vits-melo-tts-zh_en`, 1 speaker, 163 MB `model.onnx`, sample rate 44,100 Hz, plus lexicon.txt 6.5 MB and small FSTs. "If you input English words, only those that are present in the lexicon.txt can be pronounced." Raspberry Pi 4 Model B Rev 1.5 RTF: 6.727 / 3.877 / 2.914 / 2.518 at 1/2/3/4 threads. The benchmark binary is `sherpa-onnx-offline-tts` (offline, not a streaming latency figure). — [sherpa-onnx VITS page](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/vits.html)
- ONNX export is not a first-class command in the MeloTTS README. Maintainers point at sherpa's `scripts/melo-tts/export-onnx.py`. A converted Chinese ONNX was reported as 163 MB. — [MeloTTS issue 164](https://github.com/myshell-ai/MeloTTS/issues/164)
- A 17 Oct 2026 blog (updated 20 Nov 2026) claims a comparison table of "中文 MOS 4.2" and "CPU RTF 0.05", then its own PyTorch wall clock on an unnamed CPU is about 1.50 s for 1.67 s of 24 kHz audio on the first call and about 955 ms (RTF 0.57) warm, of which BERT ~380 ms and HiFi-GAN ~470 ms. It also quotes an unretrieved `season-studio/MeloTTS-ONNX` note: i5-12500H FP32 RTF 0.18 / INT8 0.06, and an INT8 model of 55 MB. The 24 kHz figure conflicts with sherpa's 44,100 Hz for the ONNX export. The 0.05 RTF and MOS 4.2 conflict with the same post's own 0.57 measurement. Treat the MOS, INT8 sizes, and i5-12500H table as unverified secondary claims, not as official benchmarks. — [xuqi2024 MeloTTS writeup](https://xuqi2024.github.io/2026/10/17/2026-10-17-audio-15-melotts/)
- Implementation: Python + PyTorch for the upstream library; ONNX Runtime if the sherpa export is used. RAM was not published.

#### Kokoro-82M standalone (hexgrad), v1.1-zh

- `hexgrad/Kokoro-82M` (v1.0, 27 Jan 2025): 82 million parameters, StyleTTS 2 decoder + ISTFTNet, no diffusion, no encoder released. "Apache-licensed weights." v1.0 training data "few hundred hrs", 8 languages and 54 voices. Sample-rate line was not in the v1.0 excerpt; v1.1 usage writes wavs at 24,000 Hz. — [hexgrad/Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M)
- `hexgrad/Kokoro-82M-v1.1-zh` (26 Feb 2025): "a short training run that added 100 Chinese speakers from a professional dataset" granted by LongMaoData, plus 3 hours of synthetic English (Maple, Sol, Vale). "This model is not a strict upgrade over its predecessor since it drops many voices." Release table: v1.1-zh, ">100 hours", "2 & 103" languages and voices. "Languages: English, Chinese." "82 million parameters, same as Kokoro-82M." The card body excerpt does not repeat the Apache sentence; a third-party license table attributes Apache-2.0 to this checkpoint and to the onnx-community export. — [hexgrad/Kokoro-82M-v1.1-zh](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M-v1.1-zh); [MiniSpeech licenses](https://minispeech.com/licenses/)
- sherpa sample page for the same checkpoint: 103 speakers, 24,000 Hz. Prefixes: af American female 2, bf British female 1, zf Chinese female 55 (sid 3–57), zm Chinese male 45 (sid 58–102). — [sherpa kokoro-multi-lang-v1_1](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/all/Chinese-English/kokoro-multi-lang-v1_1.html)
- Official `samples/make_zh.py` is hardcoded, sets `SAMPLE_RATE = 24000`, uses `KPipeline(lang_code='z')` plus an `en_callable` for English tokens, and comments "HACK: Mitigate rushing caused by lack of training data beyond ~100 tokens." — [make_zh.py](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M-v1.1-zh/blob/main/samples/make_zh.py)
- kokoro-onnx release `model-files-v1.1` (file contract note dated 1 Mar 2025): `kokoro-v1.1-zh.onnx` 326 MB FP32; `kokoro-v1.1-zh.fp16.onnx` 164 MB, spectral correlation 0.994 vs fp32; `kokoro-v1.1-zh.int8.onnx` 114 MB, spectral correlation 0.874 vs fp32; `voices-v1.1-zh.bin` 53 MB for 103 voices. The release adds a duration output used for `create(continuous=True)` sliding-window synthesis. English v1.0 int8 in the same table is 114 MB with spectral correlation 0.916, so the published Chinese int8 match to fp32 is worse than English. — [thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx releases](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases)
- A different export, RapidSpeech.cpp: F16 GGUF 156 MB, q5_k_m 80 MB, q4_k_m 73 MB, q3_k_m 68 MB, q2_k_m 63 MB, 24 kHz, 82 M. The page calls q4/q5 "indistinguishable from f16" without a listening-test table. G2P is a C++ port of `misaki[zh]` v1.1: cn2an, jieba POS, tone sandhi, pypinyin, English segments via misaki English with letter-by-letter fallback. — [RapidSpeech.cpp kokoro.md](https://github.com/RapidAI/RapidSpeech.cpp/blob/main/docs/kokoro.md)
- CPU speed, not on a named i5-8260U: a Sep 2025 CSDN post reports "TTS performance RTF= 0.61" for both an English paragraph and a Chinese paragraph that includes the English name "Jacky", and says English quality is high and Mandarin is "不错", while name polyphones 任 and 单 were wrong. CPU model not stated. — [CSDN Kokoro C++ port note](https://adg.csdn.net/694d02505b9f5f31781acab8.html)
- A Sep 2025 independent note: on an unspecified laptop, ~400 Chinese-English characters took about 110 s; on CPU, ~50 Chinese characters took about 1.2 s single-thread (GPU 3090: 0.14 s). It also says the practical context is about 150 Chinese characters before quality drops, and subjectively prefers zf_022 and zm_010. Audio duration is not given, so those timings are not an RTF. They also do not match a single RTF=0.61 story, because 110 s for ~400 characters is a different regime (load time vs steady state is not separated). — [jiangsanyin TTS notes](https://jiangsanyin.github.io/2025/09/08/tts%E7%9B%B8%E5%85%B3%E6%A8%A1%E5%9E%8B%E4%B8%8E%E5%BC%80%E6%BA%90library%E6%B5%8B%E8%AF%84/)
- Mandarin is a first-class training run (100 professional speakers), not a phoneme hack, but the author calls the run short and not a strict upgrade. Mixed English is a separate G2P path (`en_callable` / misaki English), not a claim of native code-switch prosody.
- RAM: not published in the pages above. Implementation: Python (`kokoro` + `misaki`) or ONNX Runtime (`kokoro-onnx`) or C++ GGUF ports. Prefer the ONNX package over the PyTorch stack for this app.

#### KittenTTS / KittenML

- Docs page "Last updated on Nov 23, 2026": `kitten-tts-2-latest` is 1.7B ternary and is the only listed model whose languages include Simplified Chinese (also English, Japanese, Korean, and others; 172 voices on that page). `kitten-tts-2-mini` is 82M, 118 voices, 16 languages with no Chinese (en, de, es, it, pt-BR, ru, hi, da, fi, fr, ja, ko, nl, sv, sw, tr). `kitten-tts-2-nano` is 17M per language, 8 languages, no Chinese. `kitten-tts-2-pico` is 7M per language, 8 languages, no Chinese. `kitten-tts-mini-0.8` is 80M English only. — [KittenML models docs](https://docs.kittenml.com/models)
- GitHub `KittenML/KittenTTS` (created 2025-08-05): Pico "~7M" / "Just 4 MB", "Faster-than-realtime speech generation on a single smartphone CPU thread", "40–50 MB peak memory". Nano "~17M" / "Only 8.8 MB". Mini "~82M" / "48 MB", 16 languages. Ternary 1.7B "~450 MB" in the prose and "492 MB" in the model table, "115 + cloning" voices. That voice count (115) conflicts with the docs page (172). Mini/nano/pico ship int8 and int4 ONNX and run on CPU with ONNX Runtime, no PyTorch required. int8 sizes: mini 82.7 MB, nano 17.4 MB per language, pico 8.0 MB (docs) or 8 MB (readme). int4: mini 47.5 MB, nano 8.8 MB, pico 4.0 MB. Sample rate on the mini/nano HF cards: 24 kHz. — [KittenML/KittenTTS](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/); [kitten-tts-2-mini](https://huggingface.co/KittenML/kitten-tts-2-mini); [kitten-tts-2-nano](https://huggingface.co/KittenML/kitten-tts-2-nano); [docs/kitten-tts-2-tiny.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/kitten-tts-2-tiny.md)
- A LiteRT port of the *English* 15M nano 0.8 measured RTF 0.187 fp32 on a Raspberry Pi 5 (4 threads, 8 GB, 2026-08-06) and says ONNX Runtime on that device class was RTF 0.30. Not a Chinese model. — [litert-community/kitten-tts-nano-0.8](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/kitten-tts-nano-0.8)
- License text for KittenTTS weights was not retrieved. No CPU RTF for the 1.7B Chinese model was found. Small Kitten models have no Mandarin voice.

#### NeuTTS / NeuTTS Air (neuphonic/neutts)

- Supported languages on the current README: "English, Spanish, German, French (model-dependent)." No Chinese model is listed. NeuTTS-Air: ~360M active parameters, ~552M embedding+active, phoneme input, Apache 2.0, cloning, streaming "GGUF only". NeuTTS-Nano ~120M active / ~229M emb+active and NeuTTS-2E ~125M / ~236M are under "NeuTTS Open License 1.0", not Apache. ONNX exists for the codec decoder only (`neucodec-onnx-decoder` and an int8 decoder), not as a full text-to-wave graph. — [neuphonic/neutts](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/)
- Q4/Q8 size figures conflict. DeepWiki (secondary): Q4 GGUF "~300MB", Q8 "~500MB", PyTorch "~2GB", and "Q4 GGUF + ONNX" "Low (500MB)". An Oct 2025 HN comment: full model "1.5GB", "q4 quant is ~500MB". — [DeepWiki deployment notes](https://deepwiki.com/neuphonic/neutts-air/2.2-model-formats-and-deployment-options); [HN 45489311](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45489311)
- Official throughput is llama-bench tokens/s for the Q4 backbone only, codec excluded: Galaxy A25 5G CPU 20 tok/s (Air) / 45 tok/s (Nano); Ryzen 9 HX 370 119 / 221; iMac M4 111 / 195. Not an audio RTF. — [neuphonic/neutts](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/)
- Reject for this project: no Mandarin.

#### OuteTTS

- A capability table lists "OuteTTS 1.0 1B", released Apr 2025, 12 languages, cross-lingual cloning, sample rate 44.1 kHz, license "CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 + Llama 3.2", CPU column checked. #### Supertonic (supertone-oss-archive/supertonic)

- Repository archived; "Development and support have ended." Supertonic 3: ~99M parameters, ONNX Runtime, 31 languages listed by code. Chinese / `zh` is not in the list. Supertonic 2 ~66M and 5 languages; Supertonic 1 ~66M English only. Sample code MIT. Weights OpenRAIL-M (commercial use allowed with use restrictions: no impersonation, no fully automated legal decisions, no law-enforcement use, and others). README says "44.1kHz" output. A third-party table instead says 24 kHz; prefer the README. Release note 2026.04.29 for the 31-language model. An Onyx Boox Go 6 demo is described as average RTF 0.3× offline. — [supertone-oss-archive/supertonic](https://github.com/supertone-oss-archive/supertonic); [MiniSpeech licenses](https://minispeech.com/licenses/); [tts-bench](https://5uck1ess.github.io/tts-bench/capabilities.html)
- Reject: no Mandarin, and the project is archived. OpenRAIL-M would also need a legal read before any product use.

#### Pocket TTS (kyutai-labs/pocket-tts)

- GitHub license field: MIT. Official highlights: CPU, "100M parameters, and English models down to 1.9M", audio streaming, "~200ms to get the first audio chunk", "~6x real-time on a CPU of MacBook Air M4", "Uses only 2 CPU cores", PyTorch 2.5+, voice cloning. Official languages: English, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Spanish, Dutch. Small English checkpoints named in the README: 18.2M, 6.9M, 1.9M (decoder included), October 2026. Non-English languages also have larger 24-layer variants. — [kyutai-labs/pocket-tts](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/)
- Same README, cloud x86 VM with 4 vCPUs and a T4: CPU "RTF ~2.3-2.5x" vs GPU "~6.28x" (their "Nx" means times realtime, i.e. RTF about 0.40–0.43 on that 4-vCPU VM). GPU path is unofficial.
- Weight license conflict: MiniSpeech says Pocket TTS code MIT and weights CC BY 4.0. tts-bench says "Pocket-TTS v4" "Apache 2.0". The README points at a per-voice license page that was not fetched. — [MiniSpeech licenses](https://minispeech.com/licenses/); [tts-bench](https://5uck1ess.github.io/tts-bench/capabilities.html)
- Community Chinese exists only as a pointer: "Pocket TTS Chinese (中文) by @hustfyb, 6 layers", with a local `demo.py` that takes `--prompt your_voice.wav` and a mixed-looking numeric sentence. No parameter count, file size, sample rate, license, RTF, or ONNX file is given for that checkpoint. — [kyutai-labs/pocket-tts](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/)
- Official runtime is PyTorch, not ONNX. MiniSpeech says it converted a small *French* Pocket model to ONNX. That does not cover Chinese.
- Reject the official package for lack of Mandarin. Do not recommend the hustfyb 6-layer Chinese add-on until someone publishes size, license, and a CPU RTF.

#### StyleTTS2-lite (dangtr0408/StyleTTS2-lite)

#### PaddleSpeech FastSpeech2 / VITS-era Chinese

- Released-model table (acoustic models, static size): FastSpeech2 CSMSC 157 MB (ONNX zip listed); FastSpeech2-CNNDecoder CSMSC 84 MB, with streaming static, streaming ONNX, and Paddle-Lite zips; FastSpeech2 AISHELL-3 147 MB; FastSpeech2 ZH_EN mix 145 MB with ONNX; male-zh 146 MB; male-mix 146 MB; Cantonese 146 MB. Parameter counts and sample rates are not in that table. — [PaddleSpeech released_model.md](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleSpeech/blob/HEAD/docs/source/released_model.md)
- Non-streaming CPU benchmark, Paddle 2.2.2, machine described as 24-core Xeon Gold 6148 (the writeup's GPU note is a separate V100 box; the CPU table is on that writeup's CPU section): fastspeech2 + mb_melgan RTF 0.2733; fastspeech2 + hifigan RTF 1.7128; fastspeech2 acoustic-only RTF 0.07090; mb_melgan vocoder-only RTF 0.1958; hifigan vocoder-only RTF 1.62216. Author conclusion: fastspeech2 + mb_melgan is the speed/quality compromise and is RTF < 1 on that CPU; hifigan is not. Test set is the last 100 CSMSC sentences. Date of the wiki page shown as 7 Nov 2022. Frontend time is excluded (they synthesize from dumped phonemes). — [PaddleSpeech TTS-Benchmark wiki](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleSpeech/wiki/TTS-Benchmark)
- Streaming ONNX benchmark, `fastspeech2_cnndecoder_onnx` + `hifigan_onnx`, ONNX Runtime 1.10.0, 4 CPU threads, `merge_sentences=True`. On an Intel Core i5-8250U (4 cores / 8 threads, 8 GB, Windows 10 laptop): HTTP first-packet 0.311 s, tail 2.353 s, audio 3.777 s, RTF 0.623, 0 late packets. WebSocket on the same machine: first-packet 0.278 s, tail 2.223 s, RTF 0.588. A server Xeon E5-2680 v4 was RTF ~0.49 with first packet ~0.22–0.23 s. This i5-8250U is the closest published CPU to an i5-8260U. — [same wiki](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleSpeech/wiki/TTS-Benchmark)
- ZH_EN mix is a separate released acoustic model (145 MB static). The RTF tables above are CSMSC Mandarin, not the mix model. Mandarin (Baker/CSMSC) is first-class. Code language is Python/Paddle with an ONNX export path. Apache license text was not opened in this pass (see Gaps).
- Vocoder file size is not in the acoustic table, so 84 MB is not the whole install. Streaming is a documented ONNX path, unlike most 2025 LLM-TTS models.

Pi 4 RTF 4.275 / 2.494 / 1.840 / 1.593 at 1/2/3/4 threads. Not realtime on a Pi 4. Lexicon + number/phone FSTs; English-in-Chinese behavior not stated. License of the training repo was not opened. — [sherpa VITS page](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/vits.html)
- Same table: `vits-zh-hf-fanchen-C` 116 MB, 187 speakers, 16 kHz; `fanchen-wnj` 116 MB, 1 male, 16 kHz; `theresa` and `eula` 117 MB, 804 speakers, 22,050 Hz; `aishell3` 116 MB, 174 speakers, 8,000 Hz. These are community/character or AISHELL exports. 8 kHz AISHELL3 is below a spoken-reply bar. Pi 4 RTFs appearing earlier in that page (about 6.06 and 6.08 at 1 thread) were not tied to a named Chinese file in the extracted lines, so they are not attributed here.

#### Bert-VITS2 and GPT-SoVITS

- `fishaudio/Bert-VITS2`: the README says the project is not being maintained in the short term and points users at Fish-Speech. No parameter count, ONNX size, or CPU RTF was on the retrieved page. — [fishaudio/Bert-VITS2](https://github.com/fishaudio/Bert-VITS2/)
- GPT-SoVITS v2 Pro / v2 ProPlus, via ailia's writeup: v2 Pro 133M+77M parameters, v2 ProPlus 152M+77M, versus v3/v4 at 330M+77M. Published RTF for v2 ProPlus: RTX 4060 Ti 0.028, RTX 4090 0.014, Apple M4 CPU 0.526 (they also say 1400 characters ≈ 4 minutes of audio in 3.36 s on the 4090). Chinese uses an extra BERT embedding. An ailia distill flag is described as alpha and about 1.8× faster inside their SDK (`--distill small` 5944 ms → 3823 ms; `--distill base` 3294 ms, on an unspecified device). That distill is not shown as a public few-hundred-MB ONNX. — [ailia GPT-SoVITS v2 Pro](https://tech.ailia.ai/gpt-sovits-v2-pro-%E9%AB%98%E9%80%9F%E3%81%8B%E3%81%A4%E9%AB%98%E7%B2%BE%E5%BA%A6%E3%81%AA%E9%9F%B3%E5%A3%B0%E5%90%88%E6%88%90%E3%83%A2%E3%83%87%E3%83%AB-81f2156366cd/)
- `baicai-1145/GPT-SoVITS-CPUFast`: inference-only fork aimed at CPU. Chinese is "still heavier" because of g2pw and BERT. A distilled HuBERT frontend is 11.9 MB and 2.97M parameters versus a 95M teacher; it replaces only the SSL frontend. Their Chinese end-to-end wall time improved from 15.136 s to 8.309 s, but the audio duration of that test is not stated, so it is not an RTF. No claim that the full stack fits in a few hundred MB or runs at RTF ≪ 1 on an 8th-gen U-series CPU. — [GPT-SoVITS-CPUFast](https://github.com/baicai-1145/GPT-SoVITS-CPUFast)
- No tiny open-weight distill of the whole GPT-SoVITS or Bert-VITS2 system was found. Fish-Speech was not sized in this pass; it did not appear among the small ONNX/CPU hits.

#### espeak-ng as a TTS

- Not a neural candidate. Piper's older pipeline and Kokoro's English fallback both call espeak-ng as a *phonemizer*. The Piper secondary writeup judges that Chinese G2P as rough. No MOS, sample rate, or RTF for espeak-ng's formant Mandarin voice was retrieved. Honest quality judgment from that limited evidence: usable only to debug a pipeline, not as the spoken-reply voice.

#### edge-tts / Azure

- Excluded. They are network voices, not local weights. Their repos were not re-read; there is no local open checkpoint to size.

#### 2025–2026 edge / CPU Chinese models that showed up in search

- `xiaoliukuailiu/tiny-voca-zh`, created 2026-11-21, Apache-2.0 for code and weights. "4,564,555 parameters, 18 MB", waveform generator included. Claimed speed: ">10x real time on CPU (0.44 s for 5.9 s of audio)" with the CPU unnamed. Two fixed voices, ZH_F1 and ZH_M1, Mandarin, 24 kHz. Runtimes: PyTorch, ONNX Runtime, ONNX Runtime Web. Export: `python -m tiny_voca.export`. Limits stated by the author: Mandarin only, no cloning, pypinyin errors on rare names, tone sandhi can over-apply across a whole sentence, and "Audio quality ceiling = Qwen3-TTS" because the corpus is synthetic Qwen3-TTS 1.7B VoiceDesign with "no human recordings." — [xiaoliukuailiu/tiny-voca-zh](https://github.com/xiaoliukuailiu/tiny-voca-zh)
- loom.cpp Pi 4, 4 threads, same-board comparison: "tiny-voca · TTS, 5.4 s of speech" onnxruntime FP32 0.52 s, onnxruntime int8 0.64 s, loom F32 0.40 s, loom int8 0.35 s. Implied ONNX FP32 RTF ≈ 0.52/5.4 ≈ 0.096 if those cells are wall times for that utterance (the table header is seconds). The int8 footnote marker is in the upstream table and was not expanded in the snippet. — [loom-ai-org/loom.cpp](https://github.com/loom-ai-org/loom.cpp)
- `OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS-Nano`, created 2026-04-10. GitHub license field: Apache License 2.0. Product page also says Apache 2.0. The README still ends with: "If you are reading this before that file is published, please treat the repository as not yet licensed for redistribution." Open the root LICENSE before shipping. — [OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS-Nano](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS-Nano); [moss.cn model page](https://www.moss.cn/en/models/moss-tts-nano)
- Official claims: 0.1B parameters, 48 kHz stereo, 20 languages including Chinese and English, autoregressive audio-tokenizer + LLM, streaming, "streaming generation can run on a 4-core CPU". ONNX CPU release 2026-04-17 (`MOSS-TTS-Nano-100M-ONNX` + `MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer-Nano-ONNX`): no PyTorch at inference, voice cloning kept, "nearly 2x" the original, "smooth" on 1 CPU core of a MacBook Air M4. Tokenizer described as about 20M parameters, 12.5 Hz RVQ, 16 codebooks. No official byte size and no official RTF number. Code-switching inside one sentence is not specified beyond "Chinese, English, and more." — [README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS-Nano); [README_zh](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS-Nano/blob/main/README_zh.md)
- Third-party INT8 repack `REALBITS/MOSS-TTS-Nano-100M-ONNX-int8`: upstream fp32 LM directory "640 MB"; this int8 pack "~198 MB" (shared weights 111 MB + local LM 85 MB + small graphs). Codec left fp32; they call it "42 MB". Their RTF table, CPU model unnamed: x86 4-thread fp32 0.35 vs int8 0.25; Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 fp32 0.60 vs int8 0.36; warm time-to-first-audio on that phone 123 ms. They also write "English / Chinese / Japanese only", which contradicts the official 20-language list; treat that as a limit of their notes or their build, not as proof the upstream model dropped languages. Spectral MAE "~2 dB above fp32 noise floor" is their quality check, not a MOS. — [REALBITS int8 card](https://huggingface.co/REALBITS/MOSS-TTS-Nano-100M-ONNX-int8)
- A C port aimed at Windows CPU quasi-realtime, i5-1240P, 4 threads, int8 decode + int4 frame: RTF "~0.48–0.66", hot start "~0.45 s" until ready and "~1.1 s" to first sound including prebuffer, model load "~5 s", working set "~900 MB". i5-1240P is a newer, faster CPU than i5-8260U. — [li-ming1/moss-tts-nano-c](https://github.com/li-ming1/moss-tts-nano-c)
- Qwen3-TTS 1.7B appears only as tiny-voca-zh's teacher. Its own download size and CPU RTF were not opened. A 1.7B teacher is outside the few-hundred-MB envelope.

### Inferences

- "CPU real-time" on a vendor README is not evidence for an i5-8260U that is also running ASR. The only laptop-class measurement in the same generation is PaddleSpeech's i5-8250U streaming test (RTF ~0.6, first audio ~0.3 s).
- Kokoro v1.1-zh is the only 2025 model that is Mandarin-first, permissively described, ONNX-quantized, and still under ~220 MB in fp16 (164 MB graph + 53 MB voices) or ~167 MB in int8 (114 + 53).
- Piper Chinese *files* are small and the English cousins are realtime on a Pi 4, but as of the October 2026 license audit every official zh_CN voice was excluded, and the November huayan card is still Lessac plus "License: Unknown". The newer chaowen card (CC0 data, hifi_tts_92 base, g2pW) may have changed that story for one voice only; this pass did not find a lawyer-ready statement of the hifi_tts_92 recording license.
- MeloTTS is the cleanest *license and code-switch* story (MIT, documented mixed Chinese-English) and the sherpa ONNX is 163 MB, but Pi 4 RTF 2.5 at 4 threads means the 44.1 kHz graph is heavy. An 8260U is faster than a Pi 4; how much faster was not measured. Lexicon OOV English is a real product limit of the ONNX export.
- tiny-voca-zh is three days old in this research window, trained on synthetic speech, and Mandarin-only. Speed is the best documented of the set (Pi 4 ONNX well under RTF 0.1). Quality is capped by the author's own words.
- MOSS-TTS-Nano is the serious 2026 streaming multilingual model, and Chinese is one of its headline languages. fp32 at 640 MB plus a ~900 MB working set on a newer laptop CPU misses the "shared ultrabook" constraint. The int8 LM (~198 MB) + 42 MB codec might fit on disk; RSS on an 8260U is unknown and the 1240P port was only RTF 0.5–0.7 with ~1.1 s to first sound.
- GPT-SoVITS v2 ProPlus can be RTF 0.53 on an M4, which does not imply RTF < 1 on an 8260U, and the Chinese path still pulls BERT and a reference encoder. The "distill" found here is either a 3M HuBERT frontend or a vendor alpha flag, not a small open TTS.

### Gaps

- No RTF, RSS, or time-to-first-audio measured on an i5-8260U (or any Whiskey Lake / Amber Lake U-series) for any model.
- Piper code LICENSE file was not retrieved. xiao_ya model card was not fetched (only the Loudness exclusion row and the 58 MB mirror size). hifi_tts_92 / Cori Samuel recording license was not opened, so chaowen's November retrain cannot be cleared for commercial use from these notes.
- MeloTTS official sample rate and parameter count were not on the README. HF file sizes for the PyTorch checkpoint were not listed. The blog's 220 MB / 24 kHz / INT8 table should not be copied into a recommendation without the `season-studio` release notes themselves.
- Kokoro v1.1-zh Apache line was seen on the v1.0 card and on MiniSpeech's attribution, not as a quoted YAML line from the v1.1-zh README. LongMaoData's grant is described as permissive but is not a named OSS license. No RSS figure. The two CPU timing posts do not name the CPU and disagree with each other.
- KittenTTS weight license not opened. 1.7B Chinese CPU RTF and exact int4/ternary byte size (450 vs 492 MB; 115 vs 172 voices) not reconciled from a single model card.
- OuteTTS language list not confirmed. Pocket TTS Chinese (hustfyb) has no model card in the README excerpt. PaddleSpeech LICENSE file and vocoder ONNX byte sizes and sample rate were not opened. VITS-fast-fine-tuning license not opened.
- CosyVoice, Fish-Speech, F5-TTS, IndexTTS, Spark-TTS, and Qwen3-TTS weight trees were not opened. They did not appear as sub-few-hundred-MB ONNX CPU models in the edge search. Absence from that search is not a measured size.
- Matcha-TTS Chinese was not given its own pass; published Chinese Matcha checkpoints found in passing are sherpa packages, which this task was told to skip.

## Which ones are too large, GPU-only, or Chinese-poor, and should be rejected for this device?

### Takeaway

Reject NeuTTS, Supertonic, official Pocket TTS, StyleTTS2-lite, KittenTTS mini/nano/pico, and espeak-ng because they do not speak Mandarin (or, for espeak, because it is not a neural voice). Reject KittenTTS 2 (1.7B), OuteTTS 1B, GPT-SoVITS, and Bert-VITS2/Fish-Speech-class models because they are too big, non-commercial, or have no demonstrated small CPU artifact. Reject AISHELL3 VITS at 8 kHz on quality. Treat Piper zh_CN as license-blocked until one voice is cleared. Treat full-precision MOSS-TTS-Nano as too heavy to share with ASR. Do not use edge-tts.

### Cited Findings

- KittenTTS 2 Mini (82M), Nano (17M), Pico (7M), and mini-0.8 (80M English) language lists do not include Chinese. The Chinese-capable Kitten model is the 1.7B ternary, listed at about 450–492 MB before voices, with no CPU RTF for Chinese. — [KittenML models](https://docs.kittenml.com/models); [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/)
- NeuTTS language list is English, Spanish, German, French. Air is already a ~0.5B-class GGUF (hundreds of MB to ~500 MB) plus a codec. — [neuphonic/neutts](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/)
- Supertonic 3's 31 language codes omit Chinese. The repo is archived. — [supertonic README](https://github.com/supertone-oss-archive/supertonic)
- Pocket TTS official language list omits Chinese. The community Chinese pointer has no published size or benchmark. Official speed (M4 ~6× realtime, 4-vCPU VM ~2.3–2.5×) is for the non-Chinese models. — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/)
- StyleTTS2-lite has English and Vietnamese checkpoints only; Vietnamese is CC BY-NC. — [StyleTTS2-lite](https://github.com/dangtr0408/StyleTTS2-lite)
- OuteTTS 1.0 is 1B and CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 plus Llama 3.2. — [tts-bench](https://5uck1ess.github.io/tts-bench/capabilities.html)
- Bert-VITS2 README says it is unmaintained. GPT-SoVITS v2 ProPlus is 152M+77M plus Chinese BERT, with the only CPU RTF on an M4 (0.526), and the CPU fork still reports multi-second Chinese wall times without proving RTF ≪ 1. The public "distill" is a 11.9 MB HuBERT student, not a full TTS. — [Bert-VITS2](https://github.com/fishaudio/Bert-VITS2/); [ailia](https://tech.ailia.ai/gpt-sovits-v2-pro-%E9%AB%98%E9%80%9F%E3%81%8B%E3%81%A4%E9%AB%98%E7%B2%BE%E5%BA%A6%E3%81%AA%E9%9F%B3%E5%A3%B0%E5%90%88%E6%88%90%E3%83%A2%E3%83%87%E3%83%AB-81f2156366cd/); [GPT-SoVITS-CPUFast](https://github.com/baicai-1145/GPT-SoVITS-CPUFast)
- sherpa AISHELL3 VITS is 8,000 Hz. zh-ll VITS is 16 kHz and RTF 1.59 even at 4 threads on a Pi 4, so it is not a comfortable CPU voice on a weak board; an 8260U might clear RTF 1, but that was not measured, and 16 kHz is a quality cap versus 22–24 kHz alternatives. — [sherpa VITS page](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/vits.html)
- Piper: all three official zh_CN voices were on the October 2026 non-commercial/Lessac exclusion list. huayan's live card still says Lessac and license unknown. xiao_ya is DataBaker non-commercial. — [Loudness.ai](https://loudness.ai/research/piper-tts-voices-ranked/); [huayan MODEL_CARD](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/raw/main/zh/zh_CN/huayan/medium/MODEL_CARD)
- MeloTTS ONNX is not "too large" at 163 MB, but it is not realtime on Pi 4 (RTF 2.518 at 4 threads). Rejecting it outright would be wrong; treating the README phrase "CPU real-time" as proof for this ultrabook would also be wrong. — [sherpa VITS page](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/vits.html)
- MOSS fp32 LM directory is cited at 640 MB, and a CPU port on i5-1240P used ~900 MB RSS at RTF 0.48–0.66. That combination is too large to assume on a shared 8th-gen ultrabook. — [REALBITS](https://huggingface.co/REALBITS/MOSS-TTS-Nano-100M-ONNX-int8); [moss-tts-nano-c](https://github.com/li-ming1/moss-tts-nano-c)
- Offline PaddleSpeech fastspeech2 + hifigan was RTF 1.71 on a Xeon-class CPU. Only the mb_melgan pairing, or the later streaming ONNX hifigan setup, was under 1. Do not ship the hifigan offline pairing. — [PaddleSpeech benchmark](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleSpeech/wiki/TTS-Benchmark)
- edge-tts / Azure: cloud, no local weights. Excluded by the task constraint.

### Inferences

- "Multilingual" and "edge" in 2025–2026 READMEs often mean European languages plus Japanese/Korean. Chinese has to be read off the language list, not assumed. Supertonic (31 languages), Pocket (7), Kitten mini (16), and NeuTTS all fail that check.
- A model can be rejected for license even when the ONNX is 60 MB. Piper zh_CN is the case.
- Sharing the CPU with ASR removes anything whose only comfortable numbers are on an M4, a 4090, or a phone NPU, unless a 4-thread x86 RTF also exists. MOSS's M4 "1 core is smooth" line is not that number. The i5-1240P port (RTF 0.5–0.7, 900 MB) is the cautionary one.

### Gaps

- Fish-Speech, CosyVoice2, IndexTTS2, and Qwen3-TTS were not given file-size citations here, so the rejection of those families is "no small open CPU artifact found", not a quoted gigabyte figure.
- Whether Piper 1.5 chaowen (hifi_tts_92 base, CC0 target data) is commercially usable is unresolved.
- Whether MOSS int8 RSS on 4 threads of an 8260U stays small enough is unresolved. The 900 MB figure is a different CPU and a double-quant C port.

## What are the 3-5 best fits under the size and CPU constraints, with the concrete reason?

### Takeaway

Best fits, in order: (1) Kokoro-82M v1.1-zh ONNX at fp16 or int8, (2) PaddleSpeech FastSpeech2-CNNDecoder streaming ONNX if a 2022 Baker voice is acceptable and the Apache license is confirmed, (3) MeloTTS Chinese via the 163 MB ONNX only as the MIT mixed Chinese-English option, with speed unproven on this CPU, (4) tiny-voca-zh as the tiny experimental Mandarin-only ONNX, (5) Piper zh_CN only after a license clearance, as the smallest mature VITS. MOSS-TTS-Nano int8 is the quality/streaming alternate if a later measurement shows RSS and RTF on a U-series CPU; it is not a default under the current numbers.

### Cited Findings

1. Kokoro-82M v1.1-zh, ONNX, fp16 164 MB or int8 114 MB, plus `voices-v1.1-zh.bin` 53 MB. 82M parameters, 24 kHz, English and Chinese, 55 female + 45 male Mandarin voices from a >100 hour professional set, Apache-2.0 as attributed for this checkpoint. ONNX Runtime path already matches an app that uses onnxruntime. int8 exists, with a published spectral correlation of 0.874 against fp32 (worse than the English int8's 0.916). Streaming-style sliding window is in kokoro-onnx 0.6 (`continuous=True`). Mixed English is implemented (`en_callable` / misaki), and one user sentence with "Jacky" was synthesized, but long inputs rush (official speed hack beyond ~100 tokens) and name polyphones are a reported failure. The only audio RTF found is 0.61 on an unnamed CPU. Reason it still ranks first: it is the only 2025 open Mandarin model with a quoted ONNX file under ~200 MB, a real Chinese speaker inventory, and a Python ONNX package that does not pull a training stack. — [v1.1-zh card](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M-v1.1-zh); [kokoro-onnx release](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases); [make_zh.py](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M-v1.1-zh/blob/main/samples/make_zh.py); [MiniSpeech licenses](https://minispeech.com/licenses/)

2. PaddleSpeech FastSpeech2-CNNDecoder + streaming HiFi-GAN ONNX. Acoustic static size 84 MB (CSMSC) or 145 MB (ZH_EN mix). On an i5-8250U, 4 threads, streaming HTTP RTF 0.623 and first packet 0.311 s; WebSocket RTF 0.588 and first packet 0.278 s, for ~3.8 s utterances, with zero late packets on that Windows laptop. Non-streaming fastspeech2 + mb_melgan was RTF 0.2733 on a 24-core Xeon, while non-streaming hifigan was not realtime (RTF 1.71), so the streaming graph is the one that matches the latency goal. Mandarin is the original Baker task; a mix model exists but was not the timed one. Reason: this is the only published RTF on a CPU that is actually the same class as i5-8260U. Cost: 2022 naturalness, vocoder bytes not quoted, license file not re-read, and the mix-EN model has no RTF of its own. — [released models](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleSpeech/blob/HEAD/docs/source/released_model.md); [TTS benchmark](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleSpeech/wiki/TTS-Benchmark)

3. MeloTTS Chinese, sherpa ONNX `vits-melo-tts-zh_en`, 163 MB, 44,100 Hz, MIT, one speaker, explicit mixed Chinese and English in the upstream README. ONNX export path exists. Reason to include: it is the only MIT model whose own docs lead with Chinese-English code-switch, and 163 MB fits the disk budget. Reason it is not first: Pi 4 RTF is 2.518 at 4 threads, so comfortable realtime on an 8260U beside ASR is not shown; English OOVs depend on `lexicon.txt`; the PyTorch API also runs a BERT frontend that the blog's own trace put at ~40% of a warm CPU call. Do not cite that blog's RTF 0.05 or INT8 55 MB as fact. — [MeloTTS](https://github.com/myshell-ai/MeloTTS/); [sherpa VITS](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/vits.html)

4. tiny-voca-zh. 4.56M parameters, 18 MB checkpoint, Apache-2.0, 24 kHz, two Mandarin voices, ONNX export in-tree. Author: 0.44 s for 5.9 s audio on an unnamed CPU. Independent Pi 4 ONNX Runtime timing: 0.52 s for 5.4 s speech (fp32). Reason: if the audition is acceptable, nothing else is this small and this fast while being Mandarin and ONNX. Limits that keep it off the top: repo created 2026-11-21, corpus is Qwen3-TTS synthetic with no human recordings, Mandarin only, tone-sandhi and proper-noun errors disclosed by the author. — [tiny-voca-zh](https://github.com/xiaoliukuailiu/tiny-voca-zh); [loom.cpp](https://github.com/loom-ai-org/loom.cpp)

5. Piper zh_CN medium, conditionally. ONNX about 58–64 MB, 22,050 Hz, three single-speaker voices, C++/ONNX runtime with no PyTorch. English Piper models of similar size are RTF ~0.35 at 4 threads on Pi 4, which is the right performance class, but that number is not a Chinese measurement. Reason it is last and conditional: Loudness (Oct 2026) excluded chaowen, huayan, and xiao_ya; the live huayan card is still Lessac and "License: Unknown"; xiao_ya is non-commercial DataBaker; chaowen's new card (CC0 data, finetuned from hifi_tts_92, pinyin via g2pW, Piper ≥ 1.5) might be the one voice worth a license review. Quality reputation in the secondary writeup is "good enough for short commands, mechanical for long reads." Do not ship huayan or xiao_ya on the current cards. — [huayan card](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/raw/main/zh/zh_CN/huayan/medium/MODEL_CARD); [chaowen card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rhasspy/piper-checkpoints/blob/main/zh/zh_CN/chaowen/medium/MODEL_CARD); [Loudness.ai](https://loudness.ai/research/piper-tts-voices-ranked/); [English Piper Pi 4 RTF](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/vits.html)

Not in the default five: MOSS-TTS-Nano ONNX. It is the right *shape* (0.1B, Apache-2.0 on the GitHub license field, Chinese included, streaming, ONNX Runtime CPU, 48 kHz, 2026), and a third party lists int8 LM ~198 MB + 42 MB codec with x86 4-thread RTF 0.25. The same family's fp32 tree is 640 MB, a C port measured ~900 MB RSS and RTF 0.48–0.66 with ~1.1 s to first audio on an i5-1240P, and the README still contains a "not yet licensed" sentence that contradicts the Apache badge. Hold it until LICENSE, RSS, and an 8260U-class RTF are checked. — [MOSS README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS-Nano); [REALBITS](https://huggingface.co/REALBITS/MOSS-TTS-Nano-100M-ONNX-int8); [moss-tts-nano-c](https://github.com/li-ming1/moss-tts-nano-c)

### Inferences

- For a Python backend that already has onnxruntime, the implementation order that matches the constraints is Kokoro ONNX (quality and license) and, if that voice is too rushed or the int8 correlation drop is audible, PaddleSpeech streaming ONNX (proven on a sibling CPU) or Melo ONNX (mix-EN, MIT, speed risk). tiny-voca-zh is the disk/RAM extreme and should be auditioned, not assumed. Piper is a speed fallback only after chaowen's base-model license is read.
- Mixed Chinese-English is not solved by the fastest options. tiny-voca-zh is Mandarin-only. Piper cards do not document embedded English. Kokoro and Melo do, with different mechanisms (misaki English callable vs a lexicon). PaddleSpeech has a separate ZH_EN checkpoint that was not benchmarked. MOSS supports both languages but code-switch behavior was not documented in the pages read here.
- None of the top options published an RSS figure except the MOSS C port (~900 MB), which is exactly why MOSS stays off the default list for a machine that is also running ASR.

### Gaps

- No head-to-head MOS on the same Mandarin sentences for Kokoro vs Melo vs PaddleSpeech vs Piper vs tiny-voca-zh vs MOSS. Loudness 79.6 for Huayan is a single-voice automatic score and conflicts with that study's own exclusion table.
- PaddleSpeech Apache text, vocoder ONNX size, and ZH_EN RTF are still unread.
- Kokoro RTF 0.61 has no CPU name; it cannot be compared with PaddleSpeech's 0.59 on an i5-8250U.
- First-audio latency for Kokoro, Melo ONNX, and Piper Chinese was not found. PaddleSpeech's 0.28–0.31 s first packet and Pocket's ~200 ms (non-Chinese) and MOSS's 123 ms (phone, third-party int8) / ~1.1 s (i5-1240P C port) are the only latency numbers, and they are not on one device.
